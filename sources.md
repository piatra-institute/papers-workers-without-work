## Bibliography

Augé, M. (1995). *Non-Places: Introduction to an Anthropology of Supermodernity* (J. Howe, Trans.). Verso. — French original *Non-lieux* (Seuil, 1992); the 2008 second edition retitles to *An Introduction to Supermodernity*. Definition verified verbatim at pp. 77–79. Supports §7's species-relativity move.

Barua, M. (2021). Infrastructure and non-human life: a wider ontology. *Progress in Human Geography*, 45(6), 1467–1489. — Crossref. Supplies both halves of the double-infrastructure claim: life cast as infrastructure, and infrastructure repurposed as habitat against design intent.

Beazley, E. (1966). The pigeon towers of Iṣfahān. *Iran*, 4, 105–109. — British Institute of Persian Studies; volume 4, no issue number. The only academic source for the tower economy; niche dimensions, the Chahār Burj capacity, the manure tax.

Blazich, F. A. (2021). Notre Cher Ami: the enduring myth and memory of a humble pigeon. *The Journal of Military History*, 85(3), 646–677. — Society for Military History; no DOI is issued by this journal. Establishes that the individuation of the hero pigeon was an Army publicity decision.

Boltovskoy, D., Guiaşu, R., Burlakova, L., Karatayev, A., Schlaepfer, M. A., and Correa, N. (2022). Misleading estimates of economic impacts of biological invasions: including the costs but not the benefits. *Ambio*, 51(8), 1786–1799. — Crossref. The published methodological critique of the invasive-species cost literature; used to qualify the billion-dollar pigeon figure.

Bureau of Land Management. (2026). *Wild horse and burro herd area and herd management area statistics*. United States Department of the Interior. — Agency data tables read directly, dated 1 March 2026: on-range 85,466 against a national high appropriate management level of 25,592.

Burnside, F. S., Plath, H. R., and Kaufmann, C. B. (1957). *Bird and rodent barrier* (U.S. Patent No. 2,777,171). United States Patent Office. — Filed 19 January 1951, granted 15 January 1957; the Nixalite porcupine-wire design, commercially sold from 1950.

Campkin, B. (2013). Placing "matter out of place": Purity and Danger as evidence for architecture and urbanism. *Architectural Theory Review*, 18(1), 46–61. — Crossref plus the author's accepted manuscript. Source for the Palmerston 1852 provenance and for Douglas's unsourced Chesterfield attribution.

Carter, A. L., Reese, D. S., van Bommel, R., van der Meer, M. T. J., and Çakırlar, C. (2026). Uncovering the lives of rock doves (*Columba livia*) in Late Bronze Age Hala Sultan Tekke, Cyprus. *Antiquity*, 1–17. — Published online 21 May 2026, First View: no volume or issue exists yet, so none is invented.

Cochrane, A. (2019). Good work for animals. In C. E. Blattner, K. Coulter, and W. Kymlicka (Eds.), *Animal Labour: A New Frontier of Interspecies Justice?* (pp. 48–64). Oxford University Press. — Chapter pagination from the OUP record; text read in the White Rose eprint. Volume print date is 12 December 2019, online January 2020; 2019 is used throughout. Carries the retirement-provision claim in §8.

Corera, G. (2018). *Secret Pigeon Service: Operation Columba, Resistance and the Struggle to Liberate Europe*. William Collins. — UK title and edition; the US edition is titled *Operation Columba*. Figures for birds dropped and returned.

Coulter, K. (2016). *Animals, Work, and the Promise of Interspecies Solidarity*. Palgrave Macmillan. — Crossref and full text. Her frame is "work done with, by, and for animals" (p. 3); the paper cites the *by* limb only, which is her own emphasis.

Darwin, C. (1868). *The Variation of Animals and Plants under Domestication* (Vol. 1). John Murray. — First edition read via Darwin Online (F877). Pigeons occupy ch. V (pp. 131–179) and ch. VI (pp. 180–223).

De Gracia, M., Cascales, M., Expert, P., Bellanger, M.-N., Le Cam, B., and Lemaire, C. (2015). How did host domestication modify life history traits of its pathogens? *PLoS ONE*, 10(6), e0122909. — Supplies the formal definition of "pestification" in its prior, plant-pathology sense.

Donaldson, S., and Kymlicka, W. (2011). *Zoopolis: A Political Theory of Animal Rights*. Oxford University Press. — Ch. 7, "Liminal Animal Denizens"; pigeons are named among the examples.

Douglas, M. (1966). *Purity and Danger: An Analysis of Concepts of Pollution and Taboo*. Routledge and Kegan Paul. — Original publisher and year confirmed (Jerolmack's own reference list gives "Penguin," which is wrong). No page locator is printed, because the passage was read in a later reprint whose pagination differs from the first edition.

Edgerton, D. (2007). *The Shock of the Old: Technology and Global History since 1900*. Oxford University Press. — UK original Profile Books, 2006; the OUP US edition of 2007 is cited. Use-centred history against the replacement narrative.

Fardon, R. (2013). Citations out of place, or, Lord Palmerston goes viral in the nineteenth century but gets lost in the twentieth. *Anthropology Today*, 29(1), 25–27. — Crossref. Douglas's biographer on the phrase's untraceable attribution.

Gering, E., Incorvaia, D., Henriksen, R., Conner, J., Getty, T., and Wright, D. (2019). Getting back to nature: feralization in animals and plants. *Trends in Ecology and Evolution*, 34(12), 1137–1151. — Crossref. The load-bearing source for ferality as something other than reversion.

Gilbert, M. T. P., and Shapiro, M. D. (2014). Pigeons: domestication. In C. Smith (Ed.), *Encyclopedia of Global Archaeology* (pp. 5944–5948). ## Considered and dropped

- **Levi, W. M. (1941). *The Pigeon*.** The standard husbandry reference, and the natural other half of the trait-inversion comparison. No copy could be opened through any route, so no husbandry quotations appear and the inversion is carried entirely by the control literature quoting itself.
- **Haag-Wackernagel, D. (1995). Regulation of the street pigeon in Basel.** *Wildlife Society Bulletin* 23(2):256–260. The citation is sound and the programme is real, but the population figures that circulate from it could not be read in the paper itself. Barcelona and Venice carry the point instead.
- **Escobar, M. P. (2014).** The power of (dis)placement: pigeons and urban regeneration in Trafalgar Square. *cultural geographies* 21(3):363–387. A good case study, dropped because the Trafalgar population counts are journalistic and the Greater London Authority holds no data from before its own byelaws.
- **Rosenberger, R. (2017). *Callous Objects*.** Cited in the literature as discussing anti-bird spikes; no such discussion could be found in the text or in reviews.
- **Nagy, K., and Johnson, P. D. (2013). *Trash Animals*.** The nearest companion volume, dropped for lack of an engaged use; a bare citation would be decorative.
- **Blattner, Coulter and Kymlicka's own chapter, "Animal Labour in a Post-Work Society"** (pp. 207–228). Directly on point by title, paywalled in fact; Cochrane's chapter in the same volume was read and is cited in its place.
- **Belgian and American pigeon-racing membership series.** Real figures exist but at inconsistent tiers; only the British association's self-reported numbers survive.
